How these reports are made

AI tools draft the text. A separate check compares every factual sentence with its source. What cannot be confirmed is cut.

IAPQB · Updated 24 November 2026

The steps

  • Topic. Each report starts from a documented problem with AI at work: a court decision, a regulator's action, a published incident or a study. Topics are fixed in batches of about 25 before drafting starts.
  • Sources. Drafts are built on primary documents where they exist: judgments, regulators' notices, standards, company statements and peer-reviewed papers. Every source is listed with its address and date, so each claim can be traced.
  • Drafting. The text is drafted with AI tools (large language models) to a fixed outline: what happened, why it matters, and what practitioners now do about it.
  • Source check. A second pass, run separately from the drafting and also done with AI tools, takes each factual sentence and checks it against the source it cites. Sentences the source does not support are corrected or removed. In the batch of 25 reports prepared in November 2026 for publication in December, 2,196 sentences were checked and 70 were removed.
  • Publication. The editor decides what is published, and IAPQB is responsible for every report under its name.
  • Corrections. If you find an error, write to contact@iapqb.org or open an issue on GitHub. Corrections are made in the report itself, and the change history of the Markdown text stays public on GitHub.

What the check does and does not do

  • It confirms that the cited source says what the sentence says. It does not confirm that the source itself is right.
  • An AI-assisted check can still miss errors. That is why every claim carries its source: please read the source before relying on a point.
  • The reports summarise practice. They are not legal advice.

Why we work this way

IAPQB's exam tests whether a person can use AI on a task and check what it produced. These reports are made the same way: AI drafts, the claims are checked against their sources, and what cannot be confirmed is left out.
