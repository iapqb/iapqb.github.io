How much do workplaces use AI?

What official surveys of firms and workers show, and why their figures differ

IAPQB Report No. 52 · Research digest · Published 24 November 2026
International AI Practice Qualification Board (IAPQB)

In one minute

  • The US Census Bureau found 3.7% of firms using AI in September 2023 and 10.0% two years later. A broader question gave 17.3% in November 2025, and 24.2% in late October 2026 under the new name "Super Intelligence" [1, 2, 3, 4].
  • For late 2025 and early 2026, Census Bureau data show 18% of US firms using AI, or 32% weighted by employees. A worker survey found 43% using generative AI for their job [5].
  • In 2025, 19.95% of EU enterprises with 10 or more employees used AI, and 21.8% of people in work used generative AI for work. Korea counted 32.9% of businesses using "AI technologies and services" in 2024 [6, 7, 8].
  • Statistics offices flag series breaks, and start a new series when a question changes. Researchers report rates per firm and per employee, and surveys list what counts as AI and ask about workers' use [1, 5, 6, 9, 10].

1. A familiar situation

A human resources manager is preparing a slide on AI use for a training budget meeting. One source says about one in five US firms use AI. Another says more than four in ten US workers use generative AI for their job [5]. Both come from serious surveys, and the slide has room for one number.

2. Why the figures differ

A business survey counts each firm once, and most firms are small. In the United States, 95% of firms have fewer than 50 employees but employ 26.6% of workers, while firms with 250 or more employees are 0.9% of firms and employ 56.2% [11]. Large firms use AI more, so the share of firms using AI is lower than the share of workers at AI-using firms, which weighting by employees measures [11, 12]. A worker survey counts people and can catch use that employers do not know about [12]. Wording matters too: which uses count, whether technologies are listed, the reference period, and who answers [5, 13].

IAPQB view. In these cases, survey design moved the published figure as much as the spread of AI did. When the US wording changed, the figure jumped 7.3 percentage points in eight weeks, more than in the previous two years (6.3 points) [2, 3]. Figures that looked far apart came closer once similar firms, weighting and questions were compared [5, 13].

3. Four cases

3.1 United States, 2023 to 2026: one question, three wordings

From September 2023, the Census Bureau's Business Trends and Outlook Survey (BTOS) asked businesses every two weeks whether they had used AI "in producing goods or services" [1, 2]. The share saying yes rose from 3.7% in September 2023 to 10.0% in late September 2025 [2]. In testing, some respondents first answered no, although they used AI in hiring, accounting or project management [1]. From 17 November 2025 the survey asked about AI "in any of its business functions". The first result was 17.3%, and because of this level shift the Bureau started a new series [1, 3].

Executive Order 14434 of 29 September 2026 told federal agencies to say "Super Intelligence" in place of "Artificial Intelligence", keeping the legal definition [4]. The questionnaire fielded from 19 October 2026 uses the new term and new examples, and asks whether employees used generative tools [10, 14]. In that survey, 24.2% said yes (24.6% two weeks earlier) and 26.8% reported use by employees. "Do not know" answers rose from 9.6% to 13.9%, with no explanation in the Census pages read [3].

3.2 United States, 2025 to 2026: firms and workers

A BTOS supplement in late 2025 and early 2026 aimed mainly to compare worker and firm views [14]. A revised Census working paper reports that 18% of firms used AI in a business function, or 32% weighted by employment. Workers used AI in tasks in 23% of firms, which account for 41% of employment. In 36% of firms where workers used AI, there was no sign of formal use by the firm. The firm's respondent answered for the workers [5].

The Real-Time Population Survey, an online survey of workers run by researchers, found 43% of US workers using generative AI for their job in January and February 2026 [5, 13]. Weighted by employment, firms that reported workers using generative AI covered 38.9% of jobs [5].

3.3 European Union, 2025: firms and people asked separately

Eurostat's survey of 157,000 enterprises in early 2025 found that 19.95% of EU enterprises with 10 or more employees used at least one of eight listed AI technologies, including 55.03% of large ones [6]. Firms with under 10 employees and the financial sector are left out [6, 13]. A household survey first asked about generative AI in 2025 [15]. Of people aged 16 to 74, 32.66% had used it in the previous three months, and 21.8% of people in work had used it for work [7, 15].

Bick and co-authors found European worker adoption only 4 percentage points above firm adoption on average. The share of European firms using AI for any purpose averaged five times the share using it for production, which they link to the low US figure before November 2025 [13].

3.4 Korea, 2024: what the count includes

Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT surveys about 12,500 businesses with 10 or more employees each year, for approved national statistics also sent to the OECD [8]. For end-2024 it reports that 32.9% used "AI technologies and services", including 66.9% of businesses with 250 or more employees and 63.1% in finance and insurance. Among users, 65.1% used free services, and the most common purpose was company security [8]. In the OECD database, Korea's figure rose from 2.65% in 2021 to 27.99% in 2022, marked as a series break that neither source explains [8, 9]. An OECD report of May 2025 highlights the need for greater international comparability in surveys of AI [16].

4. How practitioners handle it now

A changed question starts a new series. The Census Bureau began a new series after the 2025 rewording and kept the old figures in a separate table [1]. The OECD database and Eurostat flag series breaks and differing definitions, for example for Korea, Japan and the Netherlands [6, 9].

Figures are given per firm and per employee. The Census paper reports its main rates both ways [5]. A Federal Reserve Board note names the BTOS as the best source on firms' adoption, and the Real-Time Population Survey on workers' use of generative AI [11].

Surveys list what counts as AI. Eurostat asks about eight named technologies [6]. Bick and co-authors note that a list reduces ambiguity but misses unlisted tools, and that the eighth item, added in 2025, may explain part of that year's rise [13]. The BTOS gives examples, updated in October 2026 [1, 10].

Surveys say who should answer. Eurostat's model questionnaire asks for "a decision maker with major responsibility for ICT-related issues" [13]. In the Census data, firms reported AI use at 21% when owners or top executives answered, and 11% when finance and accounting staff did [5].

Firm and worker measures sit side by side. Since October 2026 the BTOS asks every two weeks whether employees used generative tools [10, 14]. Eurostat's household survey asks people about generative AI use for work [7, 15].

5. What to take from this

For people who quote these figures. A figure on AI at work needs four labels: firms or workers, the weighting, the population and the question. Without them, 18% of firms and 43% of workers look like a contradiction [5].

For managers. A national share of firms mostly describes small firms [11]. A large firm is better compared with large firms: 55.03% of large EU enterprises and 66.9% of large Korean businesses used AI [6, 8]. Asking only about the firm's own use can miss staff use [5].

For schools, training providers and employers. In the EU, 21.8% of people in work used generative AI for work in 2025 [7]. Among EU enterprises that had considered AI but did not use it, the most common reason was lack of relevant expertise (70.89%) [6].

6. Limits of this report

The figures come from different questions, populations and periods, and this report does not combine them. The Census paper used is the October 2026 revision, and such papers have not had the review given to Bureau publications [5]. The NBER paper is a working paper, and the 43% figure comes from an online survey run by researchers [5, 13]. The first "Super Intelligence" result covers two weeks. Eurostat plans to update its enterprise article on 15 December 2026 [6]. No comparable official figure for India was found.
