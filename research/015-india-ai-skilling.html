India's AI skills push, from sign-up to certificate

What public AI skilling programmes report at each step, and how employers and colleges use them

IAPQB Report No. 15 · Practice report · Published 17 November 2026
International AI Practice Qualification Board (IAPQB)

In one minute

  • On 7 March 2024, India's Cabinet approved the IndiaAI Mission with a budget outlay of Rs 10,371.92 crore (1 crore is 10 million) over five years. Its FutureSkills pillar covers AI courses, student fellowships and Data and AI Labs [1, 2, 3, 4].
  • On FutureSkills PRIME, run by the IT ministry with NASSCOM, more than 34 lakh people (1 lakh is 100,000) had registered by August 2026. More than 13 lakh had completed a course or been certified [5].
  • In SOAR's AI courses on the Skill India Digital Hub, 5,17,477 learners had enrolled and 1,00,664 had been certified by 6 August 2026 [6].
  • Companies help write courses and host apprentices, and colleges agree to take some courses into their curricula. Placements are not monitored under PMKVY 4.0, a scheme with AI job roles [7, 6, 5, 8].

1. A familiar situation

A hiring manager at an IT services company is shortlisting graduates for a junior data role. Three CVs list AI certificates: a free short government course, a FutureSkills PRIME course and an AI job role from a national training scheme. The manager wants to know what each shows: whether the person passed an assessment, at what level, and whether it can be checked.

2. Why the counts differ

The Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE) notified an AI skilling framework on 25 September 2023 with three tiers: "AI for All, AI for Many and AI for the Few" [6]. A free 4.5-hour course and a one-year vocational course both count as AI skilling [9, 10]. Programmes count people at several steps: registered, enrolled, trained, assessed and certified. Each step gives a smaller number, and labels are not always used the same way. Figures are as published, in Indian digit groups: 5,17,477 is 517,477.

In August 2026, MSDE told Parliament that "A specific assessment of demand for AI-related skills and the current workforce gap has not been done". It described its schemes as "demand-driven and industry-aligned" [10].

IAPQB view. Wherever the programmes below count people step by step, each count is smaller than the one before it, as on FutureSkills PRIME: more than 34 lakh registrations, more than 13 lakh completions or certificates [5]. Colleges and employers can check the course and the certificate: each SOAR micro-credential has a published NSQF level and credit value, and SIDH certificates can be verified [11, 12].

3. Three programmes

3.1 New Delhi, 2024 to 2026: an AI mission with a skills pillar

On 7 March 2024, the Union Cabinet approved the IndiaAI Mission "with a budget outlay of Rs.10,371.92 crore" [1]. The IT ministry (MeitY) said the money was "slated over the next five years" [2], and gave the same figures in August 2026 [3]. The mission's FutureSkills pillar is to add AI courses at undergraduate, master's and PhD level, and to set up Data and AI Labs in smaller (Tier 2 and Tier 3) cities [1].

In December 2025, the IT minister told Parliament that the government "is supporting 500 PhD scholars, 5,000 postgraduates and 8,000 undergraduates for AI related work" [13]. He said 27 labs had been set up through NIELIT, a national IT training institute [13, 5]. By March 2026, the plan was 570 labs, including 543 in Industrial Training Institutes (ITIs) and polytechnics [14]. In July 2026, MeitY said the 543 had been approved, and that 686 fellowships had been awarded across 178 institutions [4]. In August 2026, it again reported 27 NIELIT labs established [5]. No answer explains how the two fellowship figures relate.

3.2 India, 2020 to 2026: one platform, three counts

MeitY runs FutureSkills PRIME with NASSCOM, the National Association of Software and Service Companies, for training in new technologies such as AI [5, 15]. On 12 August 2026, the minister told Parliament that more than 34 lakh candidates had registered on it. More than 23 lakh had enrolled or trained, and more than 13 lakh had completed a course or been certified [5]. In December 2025, the ministry had said "more than 15.78 lakh candidates have benefitted", and its annex counted them as enrolled or trained [15].

Since November 2025, the platform has also hosted YUVA AI for ALL, a free 4.5-hour course. Every learner who completes it gets a Government of India certificate [9].

3.3 India, 2023 to 2026: the Skill India Digital Hub

MSDE launched the Skill India Digital Hub (SIDH) on 13 September 2023 as one platform for training, assessment, certification and jobs [16]. By February 2026, it had onboarded more than 1.5 crore candidates and over 68,000 employers or industry partners [17]. MSDE's Skilling for AI Readiness (SOAR) courses started on SIDH on 22 July 2025. By 6 August 2026, they had 5,17,477 learners enrolled and 1,00,664 certified [6].

SIDH also records AI job roles under MSDE's Pradhan Mantri Kaushal Vikas Yojana (PMKVY), a training scheme whose fourth version, PMKVY 4.0, has run since 2022-23 [8]. Up to 30 June 2026, 53,449 candidates had enrolled in these roles, 39,104 had been trained or oriented, 28,345 assessed and 25,466 certified [16]. A later answer for the same date called the 53,449 "trained/oriented" [10]. Under PMKVY 4.0, "placements are not monitored" [8].

4. How practitioners handle it now

Courses are tied to national standards. FutureSkills PRIME says its courses and NASSCOM certifications are aligned with National Occupational Standards and the National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF), India's scale of skill levels [18]. SOAR courses come from awarding bodies recognised by NCVET, the vocational training regulator, and MSDE's examples range from NSQF level 2 to 5.5 [11, 8]. These bodies are expected to obtain industry validation for their qualifications [8].

Colleges agree to take courses into their curricula. NASSCOM's sector skills council has signed agreements with 17 state governments and more than 2,000 academic institutions. The agreements are for adopting FutureSkills PRIME courses into curricula under the National Credit Framework [5]. SOAR's 35 micro-credentials have defined credit values [11]. AICTE-affiliated colleges, universities and ITIs deliver PMKVY 4.0 AI training [10].

Certificates can be checked. SIDH provides "Digitally Verifiable Credentials in compliance with open standards", which can be shared and verified [12]. It is linked to DigiLocker, and certified candidates are hired through two linked job portals, the National Career Service and JobX [17, 12].

Companies write and teach courses, and take apprentices. Microsoft, HCL Technologies and NASSCOM are content providers for SOAR courses [7]. With Microsoft and the Edunet Foundation, MSDE ran a one-year Artificial Intelligence Programming Assistant course at 23 National Skill Training Institutes in 2025-26, with 600 places [10]. Under the National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme, which promotes industry-led, on-the-job learning, 3,175 apprentices were engaged and 855 certified in AI and machine learning roles in the last three years [6].

Jobs after training are measured separately. PMKVY 4.0 does not monitor placements [8]. An independent evaluation of the whole scheme by the Arun Jaitley National Institute of Financial Management surveyed short-term trainees. The share who were employed or self-employed rose from 26.6% before training to 45.4% after [19].

5. What to take from this

For people who take these courses. YUVA AI for ALL gives a certificate for completing a 4.5-hour course [9]. PMKVY 4.0 reports certification as the step after assessment [16]. A CV entry with the course name, awarding body, NSQF level and SIDH credential gives an employer facts to check [11, 12].

For employers and managers. Most official counts in these programmes stop at the certificate, and PMKVY 4.0 does not monitor placements [5, 6, 8]. The employers in these cases help write courses, host apprentices and hire certified candidates through portals linked to SIDH [7, 6, 12].

For colleges and training providers. A college adopting SOAR courses can check each micro-credential's published NSQF level and credit value [11]. By July 2026, 543 labs in ITIs and polytechnics had been approved [4]. The 27 labs reported as established in August 2026 were NIELIT labs [5]. A college planning around a lab or fellowship can ask for its status in writing.

6. Limits of this report

Almost all figures come from government answers to Parliament and other releases published by the Press Information Bureau. No independent count or audit of these AI figures was found. India's national auditor's 2025 report on PMKVY covers July 2015 to March 2022 [20]. Accounts of employer and college use are official descriptions. Figures change between answers, and labels vary [16, 10]. FutureSkills PRIME counts are not broken down by course, so the share of short courses is not known. SOAR began with awareness courses for school students and educators, and its totals include them [6, 14]. The newest figures found date from August 2026.
