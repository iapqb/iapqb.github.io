# Deepfakes in election campaigns

**How Korea and India used bans, labels and takedown deadlines in 2024, and what changed by 2026**

IAPQB Report No. 36 · Practice report · Published 24 November 2026
International AI Practice Qualification Board (IAPQB)

## In one minute

- South Korea bans AI-made fake sound, images and video in election campaigns for the 90 days before each vote, and requires a set label at other times [1, 2].
- In the April 2024 general election, Korea's National Election Commission (NEC) recorded 389 actions on such deepfakes: 388 deletion requests, one warning and no criminal complaint. In the 2025 presidential election it recorded 10,513, three of them criminal complaints [3].
- On 6 May 2024, the Election Commission of India (ECI) told parties to remove deepfake and false content within three hours of notice [4]. By 2026 it required labels and action within three hours of notice to platforms [5].
- Between them, the two countries use ban periods, removal deadlines, fixed labels, detection checked by people and duties for parties [1, 2, 4, 5, 6].

## 1. A familiar situation

A week before polling day, a member of a party's social media team sees a video in a supporters' group chat. It seems to show the rival candidate admitting to bribery, looks real and has been forwarded many times. The staff member must decide fast whether it is real, who must remove it, and what to tell the election authority.

## 2. Why it happens

Generative AI has made realistic fake audio and video easier to produce. The US National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) notes that deepfakes "can challenge our ability to distinguish human-generated content from AI-generated synthetic content" [7].

Korea's NEC says deepfakes can do candidates damage that is hard to undo and can distort voters' judgement, and that the technology spreads too fast for one agency to respond alone [3]. The people who post need not be party members: Korea's ban covers anyone, and two cases below involve channel operators [1, 8, 9].

> **IAPQB view.** In these cases the main tool was the removal request. Korea's NEC recorded 388 such requests over deepfakes in 2024 and no criminal complaint; in 2025 it recorded 10,510 and three complaints [3]. India set three-hour limits, for parties in 2024 and from notice to platforms by 2026 [4, 5]. The work fell on whoever could act within hours: party staff, platforms and election monitors [4, 5, 6].

## 3. Four cases

### 3.1 South Korea, 2024: a 90-day ban and 388 deletion requests

An amendment of 28 December 2023 added Article 82-8 to the Public Official Election Act. From 90 days before election day, no one may make, edit, distribute, screen or post, for campaigning, AI-made fake sound, images or video that are hard to tell from reality. The penalty is up to seven years in prison or a fine of 10 million to 50 million won. At other times a label is required; leaving it off can bring an administrative fine of up to 10 million won [1]. The NEC's rules set the label: a fixed sentence saying the content is AI-made and not real, in a box of at least a tenth of the image or video, or spoken at the start and end of audio [2].

For the election of 10 April 2024, the ban applied from 29 January [10]. Judging detection technology to be imperfect, the NEC set up three checks: a visual check, general-purpose software and review by AI advisers. A label, it warned, did not make a deepfake lawful inside the 90 days [6]. It later counted 389 actions: 388 deletion requests, one warning and no criminal complaint [3].

### 3.2 India, 2024: three hours for parties

On 6 May 2024, with two of seven polling phases done [11], the ECI issued directions to parties on social media. Noting "certain violations" of the Model Code of Conduct and the law, it pointed parties to laws in force, including the IT Rules 2021. Parties were to remove deepfake and false content "within three hours of bringing it to their notice", warn the person responsible, report unlawful content and fake accounts to platforms, and take persistent problems to the Grievance Appellate Committee. The release gave no figures on compliance [4].

### 3.3 South Korea, 2025 and 2026: more deletion requests and the first criminal complaints

In the presidential election of 3 June 2025, the NEC recorded 10,513 actions on deepfakes: 10,510 deletion requests and three criminal complaints [3]. On 29 May 2025 it filed the first complaints under Article 82-8, against three people. One allegedly posted images such as a candidate in prison clothes. Another allegedly posted ten videos in news format, read by an AI-made presenter. The NEC notes that these allegations are not confirmed by a court [8].

In December 2025, ahead of the local elections of 3 June 2026, a local commission imposed the first fines for missing labels, 5 million won each on the maker and the sharer of an AI-made song praising a local government head [12, 13]. The NEC ran a team of about 440 people [13] and worked with police, prosecutors and platforms such as Naver, Kakao, Google (YouTube) and Meta [3]. On 1 June 2026 it filed a complaint against a channel operator over deepfake videos of six candidates, saying the operator had ignored repeated deletion requests [9].

### 3.4 India, 2025 and 2026: labels, and a deadline for platforms

For the Bihar election, the ECI told parties, candidates and star campaigners on 9 October 2025 to label AI-generated or synthetic content prominently [14]. For five assembly elections in 2026, it said it had directed that misleading or unlawful AI content be acted on within three hours of platforms being told, and that labelled content disclose the originating entity. Over 11,000 posts or links were acted on from 15 March to 19 April 2026; the release does not say how many were AI-made [5]. IT Rules amended on 10 February 2026 make platforms label permitted AI content with traceable metadata, and remove unlawful content within three hours of a court order or reasoned government notice [15].

## 4. How practitioners handle it now

**A ban period, and set labels outside it.** Korea bans anyone from campaigning with AI fakes in the 90 days before a vote, labelled or not [1, 6]. Outside that period it sets the label's wording and size, and has fined people for leaving it off [2, 12]. India sets label terms and asks for the originating entity [5]; its IT Rules add traceable metadata [15].

**A deadline for removal.** India gave parties three hours from notice in 2024, and platforms three hours by 2026 [4, 5, 15]. In Korea, whoever receives a commission's deletion request must follow it without delay [1].

**Detection checked by people.** The NEC paired detection software with human review, judging the tools imperfect [6].

**Duties for parties and candidates.** India made parties remove content, warn the member responsible and report fake accounts [4]. For Bihar, candidates also had to declare their authentic social media accounts, and political adverts needed pre-certification [16].

**Criminal complaints when removal fails.** Korea's criminal complaints are rare next to its deletion requests [3]; one in 2026 targeted an operator said to have ignored them [9]. Police cyber units were to track down the first maker and spreader [13].

## 5. What to take from this

**For people who make or share campaign content.** Check the calendar first. In Korea, realistic AI fakes for campaigning are banned in the 90 days before a vote, even with a label, and need the official label before that [1, 2, 6]. A Korean commission fined a person who forwarded an unlabelled AI song to a group chat [12].

**For party and campaign managers.** Name the person who removes content and tracks the deadline; India's three hours ran from the moment the party was told [4]. Keep a list of authentic accounts [16], and warn members whose content is removed [4].

**For election bodies and platforms.** Plan for volume and for refusal: Korea's deepfake deletion requests rose from 388 in 2024 to 10,510 in 2025, and in a 2026 case repeated requests were reportedly ignored [3, 9].

## 6. Limits of this report

The NEC's bulletin of 10 April 2024 gave 384 deletion requests, one warning and two requests to comply [17]; this report uses its later table [3]. The 2024 and 2025 figures cover different elections, and in 2024 the ban ran for less than 90 days [3, 10]. No court decisions on the complaints were found. The ECI website could not be opened, so ECI texts were read in Press Information Bureau releases, and the Gazette text of the IT Rules amendment was not read. No figures were found on how fast Indian parties removed content, or on effects on voting. Translations from Korean are IAPQB's own.
