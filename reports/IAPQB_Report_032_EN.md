# Korea's AI Basic Act at work

**Who must give notice, label AI output and name a local representative, and what the grace period means**

IAPQB Report No. 32 · Rule explainer · Published 24 November 2026
International AI Practice Qualification Board (IAPQB)

## In one minute

- Korea's AI Basic Act and its decree took effect on 22 January 2026, with amendments from 21 July 2026 [1, 2, 3]. Its duties fall on those who develop or provide AI; staff using AI as a work tool are "users" [1, 4].
- Providers of high-impact or generative AI must tell users in advance, and generative AI output needs a label. Administrative fines of up to 30 million won cover only missing notice, a missing local representative and ignored orders [1, 3].
- The science ministry (MSIT) deferred investigations and fines for at least one year, and this report found no official end date [5, 6].
- Under MSIT's guideline, hiring AI is high-impact when no person with proper authority intervenes in substance [7]. By August 2026, two outlets had reported only one foreign firm with a completed representative designation [8, 9].

## 1. A familiar situation

An HR manager in Seoul must decide whether to renew an AI video-interview service. A marketing colleague asks whether her chatbot-made images now need a label. Both know the new AI law can bring fines of up to 30 million won. Neither knows whether it applies to them or only to the tool sellers.

## 2. Why the answer depends on the role

The Act places duties on "AI business operators": developers, and businesses that provide AI products or services built on a developer's AI. Those provided with such products are "users"; anyone whose life, physical safety and fundamental rights are significantly affected is an "impacted person" [1, Art. 2]. MSIT says the transparency duty "does not apply to users who simply use AI technologies or services as tools for work or creative activities" [4].

High-impact AI is AI that may significantly affect life, physical safety and fundamental rights in listed areas, among them "hiring and loan screening" [1, Art. 2]. A missing label can lead to an investigation and a correction order, with a fine only if the order is ignored [1, Arts. 40 and 43].

> **IAPQB view.** In MSIT's hiring examples, one system is high-impact and another is not, because only in the second does a person with proper authority review the results [7]. The guideline calls the hiring company a "user" without high-impact duties, yet that company's process decides the category [7].

## 3. Four cases

### 3.1 Seoul, 2026: a law in force, with fines on hold

The Act was promulgated on 21 January 2025 and took effect with its decree on 22 January 2026 [1, 5]. Some amendments of 20 January 2026, such as provisions on "AI-vulnerable groups", took effect on 21 July 2026 [1, 2]. The decree's standard fines run from 5 million won for a first missing notice to 30 million won for a third or later ignored order, with 20 million won for a missing representative [3, Annex 2]. MSIT promised "a grace period of at least one year", in which "fact-finding investigations and the imposition of penalties will be deferred", with investigations only in "highly exceptional cases" such as loss of life [5]. On 6 November 2026 the Presidential Council on National AI Strategy set a top aim for AI safety and trust laws: make the current Act work better, with a guidance period early in enforcement. It listed a pending bill to defer AI business regulation by three years [6].

### 3.2 Seoul, 2026: labels are the provider's job

MSIT's transparency guidelines of 22 January 2026 put the duty on the business that finally provides the AI service to users, foreign firms included [4, 10]. A film company that makes films with AI video tools is a user with no duty [4], and the decree can exempt AI used only for the operator's internal work [3, Art. 23]. For office tools such as Gemini in Google Workspace and Copilot in Word, a logo or notice before use is enough unless outputs are synced and saved as files [10]. Downloaded text is marked in its header or metadata; clipboard copies are not covered. Deepfakes that leave the service need labels people can perceive [10].

### 3.3 Seoul, 2025 to 2026: hiring tools and the human check

In a 2025 government survey of the 500 largest firms, 86 of 396 respondents (21.7%) used AI in hiring, and 57.0% of those told applicants in advance that AI was used [11, 12]. The Ministry of Employment and Labor planned notice and anti-discrimination rules for the Fair Hiring Procedure Act [11]; the version in force on 16 November 2026 has no AI provision [13]. MSIT's high-impact guideline makes a hiring system high-impact when no person with proper authority intervenes in substance, or when that step is a formality [7]. A system that scores applicants and sends acceptance and rejection notices is high-impact; one that writes and marks tests under an HR officer's review and approval is not [7]. In the guideline's hiring chain, the developer and interview-system vendor carry the Article 34 duties of risk management, explanation, user protection, human oversight and records, while the employer is a "user" and the job seeker an "impacted person" [1, Art. 34; 7].

### 3.4 Seoul, 2026: few foreign providers name a representative

A foreign AI business with no address or office in Korea needs a domestic representative if in the previous year it had at least 1 trillion won in revenue, 10 billion won in AI service revenue or 1 million average daily users in Korea, or has been fined for ignoring an order [3, Art. 29]. Yonhap reported in January 2026 that OpenAI and Google met the criteria [14]. On 24 June 2026, MTN reported that only Anthropic had completed its designation and that Google's filing had been returned for missing information [8]. ZDNet Korea reported on 7 August that Anthropic was still the only one [9].

## 4. How practitioners handle it now

**Each AI use is mapped to a role first.** MSIT's guideline gives each party in a supply chain a role, with charts for hiring, health care and loans [7].

**Providers give notice up front and label output that leaves the service.** The decree accepts notice in the terms of use, on screen or at the place of service, and labels that people can perceive, or machine-readable labels plus at least one notice [3, Art. 23]. The guideline shows examples from Scatter Lab's Zeta, Gemini, Adobe Firefly and Sora [10].

**A person with proper authority stays in each hiring decision.** A formal sign-off does not count [7]. High-impact operators must post the main points of their risk management and explanation measures, their user protection measures and the overseer's name and contact, and keep records for five years [3, Art. 27].

**Unclear cases go to MSIT in writing.** MSIT answers within 30 days, extendable once by up to 30 days, and an operator that disagrees may ask again within 10 days [3, Art. 25]. MSIT's Support Desk takes questions [15]; MSIT promised confidential and anonymous consultations [5].

**Foreign providers above the thresholds must name a representative.** Article 36 requires it [1]. Anthropic had done so by June 2026; OpenAI had not by August [8, 9].

## 5. What to take from this

**For people who use AI at work.** Under MSIT's guidance, an employee using a chatbot or office AI tool is a user with no labelling duty, and copied text needs no label [4, 10]. Disclosure in a report therefore rests on company rules or client terms. A proposed Network Act amendment would extend labelling to information providers and network users [6].

**For managers and HR teams.** In MSIT's hiring examples, the category turned on an HR officer who reviewed and approved the results [7]. Buyers of such tools can ask the vendor how it classifies the tool and who its named overseer is, and give their own reviewer power to change results [3, Art. 27; 7].

**For companies that build or sell AI in Korea.** The grace period defers fines, and the duties have applied since 22 January 2026 [1, 5]. The high-impact guideline was revised three times by April 2026, so a record of the version behind each decision is worth keeping [15].

## 6. Limits of this report

Quotes from the Act use the Korea Legislation Research Institute's English text [1]; other Korean texts were translated for this report. An amendment of 20 October 2026, in force from 21 April 2027, changes only rules on training data [16]. The guidelines changed several times; this report uses the versions posted on 16 November 2026 [15]. The representative count rests on two news reports, the latest of 7 August 2026 [8, 9]. Other laws, such as personal data rules on automated decisions, are not covered.
